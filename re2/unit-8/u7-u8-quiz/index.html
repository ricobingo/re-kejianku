Reading 1 · One Word, Two Trades

AIn a printing shop, a plate is a flat piece of metal that carries the image. In a kitchen, a plate is what dinner arrives on. Nobody confuses the two, because the rooms are so different.

BThe trouble starts when two trades share a room. A hospital and a builder both use the word bay: for the nurse it is a curtained space with four beds in it; for the builder it is the space between two columns that hold up the roof. When a new wing was being planned in one city, the drawings came back with six bays where the ward needed four. The mistake cost eleven weeks.

CBoth trades were right, and both had used the word correctly for a hundred years. Neither of them had thought to ask what the other one meant. The word looked so ordinary that it never raised a hand.

DThe hospital now keeps a one-page list of about thirty such words, and every drawing comes with it. The builders keep the same list. It is not a dictionary — it only holds the words where the two trades disagree.

EThe lesson was cheap in the end. The expensive words are not the long ones you look up. They are the short ones you think you already share.

Reading 2 · Three Hours a Year

AOn one hillside in the north, a low white flower opens only after dark, and only for about three hours, on perhaps four nights each summer. Nobody can say in April which four nights they will be.

BSo the picking team does everything else first. In March they clear the paths, because in the dark a root will break an ankle. In May they weigh and label the empty boxes, so that on the night nobody has to write anything. In June two people begin sleeping in a hut beside the field, and one of them walks the rows every evening at nine. When the first flower opens, that walker sends one message, and eleven people drive up the hill.

CThe picking itself takes three hours. The year takes four months.

DIn 2019 the message went out on a Tuesday and only six people came; the rest were at work and could not leave. Since then the team has agreed on something simple: everybody keeps four nights free in July, without knowing which four.

EA visitor once asked the oldest picker what the hardest part was. She said it was not the dark, and it was not the three hours. It was asking eleven people to hold four nights empty for a flower that might not open at all.
