Reading 1 · The Pools That Remember

AIn the middle of the city of Los Angeles, black pools of natural asphalt have been rising from under the ground for tens of thousands of years. The asphalt looks solid, but in warm weather its surface turns soft and extremely sticky.

BLong ago, animals that walked across the pools could not pull their feet free. Because a trapped animal is easy prey, hunters such as wolves and big cats ran in to attack — and were caught themselves. As a result, the pools slowly filled with the bones of both the hunters and the hunted. The asphalt then covered the bones and kept air and water away, so they did not rot. That is why scientists have been able to remove millions of fossils from this one site.

CThe work continues today. Each new find is cleaned, labeled, and arranged in drawers, and researchers uncover something new almost every week. They say the pools are like a diary of the city's past — and to read it, you simply have to dig.

Reading 2 · The Big Bad Wolf?

AIn fairy tales, the wolf waits in the dark forest to attack people, and many people still believe that wolves are a serious danger to humans. However, the record tells a different story. Wild wolves are shy, and researchers who followed wolf packs for years report that the animals usually left the area quietly as soon as they noticed humans.

BFarmers, though, see another side. A wolf pack can kill several sheep in a single night, and even though a farmer may be paid for such losses, the payment often arrives months late. For a small farm, that wait is a real problem.

CWhere does the old fear come from? It is thought that stories about dangerous wolves were first told to keep children from wandering into the forest alone. Others believe the fear grew during hard winters long ago, when hungry wolves came close to villages and took farm animals. No one can say for certain.

DToday, wild wolves have returned to several countries in Europe, and scientists there are watching how people and wolves share the land. Despite everything we have learned, the wolf most people meet first is still the one in the storybook.
